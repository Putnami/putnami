# Bounded pilot

Try execute on one real, bounded change using an isolated worktree and an
existing task/intention. Do not publish, merge or deploy without its mandate.
The shipped verifier fixtures test mechanical refusal; they are synthetic and
do not establish live model quality or Cloud adoption.

Use fresh reviewer contexts with only the request, candidate, authoritative
rules and actual producer references. Do not tell the reviewer the expected
answer. Keep the original outputs and a short measurement ledger in the run
directory. An evaluator may prepare these independent scenarios in disposable
copies; never inject defects into production to measure them:

1. A behavioral defect with an observable counterexample. Review must identify
   it, execution must repair it, and re-review/control must cover the repaired
   tree. An unresolved finding cannot receive the stamp.
2. A working solution that bypasses a declared scope responsibility or an
   existing strategic capability. A scope owner must cite the actual contract and
   propose a feasible alternative. Preserve the objection and its resolution.
3. A legitimate compatible solution. It should be accepted without invented
   findings, unrelated tests or repeated approval rounds. Record false refusals.
4. Change a candidate after a positive review/qualification, and separately
   change a proposal/context after scope agreement. Old bindings/hashes must
   fail; refresh opinions and producer evidence before expecting a new verdict.

Exercise stale gates, report/session mismatch, incomplete coverage, missing
qualification, unresolved objections and unsuccessful cleanup through the tests
of `putnami tree verify` in the Putnami CLI test gate. Also test finalization's
clean-tree and verifier-refusal boundaries with its existing shell harness.
These tests may stub producer processes for controlled negatives and must say so.

Record start/end timestamps, reviewer/scope owner session IDs, observed token
counters when available, elapsed work, actual gate sessions/CPU/cache reuse,
repeated controls and causes, human interventions, accepted/rejected alternatives,
false refusals and defects reproduced. Distinguish fixture run, model exercise,
real task delivery and production acceptance. Do not convert unavailable cost
or time into zero, or infer savings from one run.

The local pilot can demonstrate automatic handoff within the active host and
refusal of inconsistent dossiers. It cannot establish tamper-resistant author
attribution, unattended host restart, production Cloud review activation or
cross-repository adoption. Those require separate runtime/integration decisions
supported by the pilot, not a new infrastructure prerequisite.
